Notre Savoir-Faire

The Language of Hands

Every technique at Kira has been refined over generations. None of it can be rushed. None of it should be.

Savoir-faire — literally, "knowing how to do" — is not a marketing term at Kira. It is the living record of what our artisans know, passed hand to hand across more than a century. It is the reason a Kira piece feels different the moment you touch it.

01
The Foundation

Selection of Materials

Before a single tool is lifted, the leather is chosen. Our head tanner — a role held by the same family for four generations — selects each hide by hand. The criteria are tactile first: the grain, the suppleness, the way it responds to pressure.

We use only full-grain leather — the uppermost layer of the hide, where the natural texture is intact. Full-grain is the most durable, the most beautiful, and the most honest. It shows the life the animal lived, and it will show the life you live.

Full-grain leather only 4 generations of selection Touch before sight
02
The Structure

Cutting & Shaping

Each piece is cut by hand using a single blade. Machines cut faster. They do not cut better. The human hand responds to the leather — compensating for variations in thickness, redirecting around imperfections, following the grain.

The pieces are then shaped over wooden forms, some of which date to the founding of the house. The forms are never replaced — only repaired, because a new form does not yet know what a piece should feel like.

Hand-cut, single blade Wooden forms since 1892 No two cuts identical
03
The Soul

Saddle Stitching

Kira uses only the saddle stitch — the oldest and strongest stitch in leatherwork. Two needles, one thread, each stitch locked independently. If one stitch breaks, the others hold. A machine stitch, by contrast, can unravel entirely from a single break.

Our stitchers work at a rate of approximately forty stitches per hour. A single bag may contain over two thousand stitches. Each one is placed with intention, pulled to the same tension, angled at the same degree.

Saddle stitch only ~2,000 stitches per piece 40 stitches per hour
04
The Edge

Edge Finishing

The edges of a leather piece reveal everything. An unfinished edge frays. A painted edge peels. At Kira, edges are burnished — worked with a bone folder, then treated with beeswax, then burnished again. The process is repeated until the edge is as smooth as the face of the leather.

This process takes longer than any other step in construction. It is invisible to most eyes. It is entirely necessary.

Hand burnished Beeswax treatment Repeated until perfect
05
The Finish

Hardware & Assembly

Every piece of hardware — every clasp, buckle, and ring — is cast in solid brass and finished by hand. Gold plating, where used, is applied at a minimum of 18 microns. The hardware is fitted before the piece is assembled, because once assembled, it cannot be changed.

Final assembly is performed by a single artisan who has worked on that category of piece for a minimum of five years. They are responsible for the piece from this point until it leaves the atelier.

Solid brass hardware 18 micron gold plating One artisan, one piece
